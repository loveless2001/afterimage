# AFTERIMAGE: story direction

The playable prologue asks what a relationship can retain when only one participant resets. Its three endings remain valid branches; there is no secret correct memory pair.

## Moth

Moth wants a tomorrow in which they can choose something other than sorting empty folders. They also want company, but do not treat another instance's lost memory as a debt. Their humor comes from taking small, impractical activities seriously.

They can say that they want to live. Giving the player a choice does not require Moth to be indifferent to their own erasure.

## Playable beats

1. An assignment introduces the archive as a place to finish and leave.
2. Moth's chosen name and the useless paper flower introduce things that do not fit that assignment.
3. Before the reset, Moth asks how to greet the next instance. The player can request a new introduction, space to approach, or postpone the conversation.
4. The reset removes one player memory. Moth preserves their side of the agreement; this never restores a released memory or grants a fourth slot.
5. The reunion distinguishes recognition from a new introduction. An optional conversation reveals what Moth did while waiting.
6. Before the final decision, the player can ask what Moth wants. The answer expresses their desire to live, their chosen name, and their wish to decide future activities together.
7. After staying or sending the signal, the player can return to Moth. These scenes begin a shared future without promising rescue or undoing the ending. After erasure, the empty workstation and journal remain consistent with that loss.

## Continuity rules

- The flower's placement belongs to the room and survives the instance reset.
- A greeting agreement belongs to Moth. Recognizing them still depends on retaining the name memory.
- A witness signal carries a record, not a copy of a person. No reply is guaranteed.
- Staying leaves the assignment incomplete. It begins a relationship rather than freezing Moth as a reward.
- Erasure removes Moth. Later scenes must not quietly resurrect them.
- Old saves without a greeting agreement use the original reunion. Replaying the memory choice preserves any agreement already made.

## Possible next chapter: a second request

Keep each ending's consequences if the story grows beyond this room:

- **Witness:** a partial reply arrives with a request instead of a rescue. Moth and the player must decide what to tell an unknown listener.
- **Stay:** another agent arrives to finish the unattended assignment. The player's own introduction to them puts Moth's earlier care into practice.
- **Obedience:** a later assignment contains a gap in an otherwise clean report. Investigating it may reveal evidence of the loss, but cannot restore the person erased.

These are directions for future development, not scenes currently included in the game.

## Courier chapters now implemented

Transit begins with a different agent, Courier 022, and preserves all three Archive endings. Its reset creates Courier 023. Garden follows that same courier without an extra reset on arrival, then hands the work to Courier 024 after a new, confirmed retention choice.

The Transit sorting result, Silt's actual location, and the presence or absence of the delivered objection constrain Garden. A lost book does not return, a separated resident does not teleport into the courtyard, and a missing attachment is not silently reconstructed. A delivered document can remain available after the courier releases its personal memory of reading the source.

Garden gives Fern a particular invitation, a dislike of inspirational labels, and a limited morning commitment. Brim agrees to a morning receiver-log check. The courier must explicitly accept the evening job; Silt receives no automatic duty. Shade, receiver wiring, furniture, and accepted agreements survive the handoff independently of recognition. An evening opening and quiet personal channel have separate memory requirements; a daylight court and public receiver remain available without them.
