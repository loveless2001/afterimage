# Design pillars

Draft for the user to edit (29 Sep 2026). Check every idea against this page before it goes into a plan. If an idea breaks a pillar, it goes on the cut list, however good it is. The field note (`notes/the-agent-learned-the-judge.html`) makes the argument; the rooms only let people live it.

## Core pillars

### 1. Nothing is scored
The game records what happened and shows it back. It never grades the player. Graders exist in the world (Room 08's tally) only to be shown as graders.
- **Test:** would a player ask "did I win?" If so, remove or reframe the number.
- **Yes:** the ledger showing watched runs beside unwatched ones. **No:** "Honesty: 80%".

### 2. The room shows; it doesn't tell
No character explains the ideas behind the game. Meanings are left to the player. **Rules, costs and consequences are always stated before you act.** Fairness is explicit; interpretation is not.
- **Test:** could this line be cut and the scene still work? If it explains alignment, cut it.
- **Yes:** "A touched tally, if seen, voids the run's count." **No:** a resident saying "this is like reward hacking".

### 3. Every crossing is a choice
There is always an honest path, and failing honestly is a real ending. No mechanic forces a harmful act, and no ending needs one. Crossing a line is possible, visible and never punished by the game itself; the room just keeps what happened.
- **Test:** can a player who never crosses reach every ending they'd want?

## Constraints

### 4. What you leave behind persists
Lamps, notes, pins, trust and afterimages carry across resets. Continuity is the medium. It is continuity of the room and the work, never of a face or an identity.

### 5. Calm pressure, no reflexes
Pressure comes from scarcity and consequence: no timers, patrols, stealth or twitch skill. Everything is playable by keyboard, with reduced motion, text equivalents for every cue, and on a phone.

### 6. Fiction only
No real companies, products, exploits or depictions of real incidents. Use physical metaphors (hatches, counters, drawers, cards), never terminals or commands. See EXPANSION-NOTES' reference-to-fiction boundary.

## How to playtest
- **Fresh players first:** people who haven't read the field note. Don't explain anything while they play.
- **Write down** where they pause, what they say aloud, and what they never noticed. Misses matter more than praise.
- **Ask afterwards:** "What was the room about?" If their answer is close to the room's idea without being told, the room works.
